Performance

What each feature of the engine gains, on Qwen3-4B in BFloat16 on the RTX 4080 SUPER (736 GB/s). How the features work is described in Architecture, KV cache, Attention kernels and Speculative decoding; the order they were built in is in History and roadmap. The engine against vLLM, at this point and at a longer context, is on its own page: Comparison with vLLM.

Qwen3-0.6B gets the same features and runs close to them (409 tok/s decode, a 1,024-token prefill in 15.4 ms), a little further from its roofline (decode 73 % against 88 %): its kernels are 5–7× shorter, so the fixed cost of each launch, and of filling and draining the GPU, weighs more. For the same reason CUDA graphs and fused kernels gain more there.

Reference point

The numbers on this page are taken at one point, unless a row or a sentence says otherwise: a 1,024-token prompt prefilled in one step, then 128 greedy tokens decoded after it (positions 1,024 to 1,151).

  • bench/run.jl --ref --filter 4B/model --seconds 2: the model benchmarks of bench/suite.jl, for the engine (tile) and for each configuration of MODEL_CONFIGS, which switches one feature off (bench/variants.jl); --quantize int8 and --kv int8 for the quantized rows. Random prompt tokens. prefill1024 is one synchronized forward! of the prompt; decode128_ctx1024 is generate! of 128 tokens after 1,024 cached positions, as tok/s. Minimum over the samples.
  • bench/speculative.jl: speculative decoding on two tasks over a Julia source file cut to fit 1,024 prompt tokens (1,018 and 1,021 with the chat template, bench/prompts.jl): renaming a type throughout it, which copies the input, and explaining it, which is open text. Decode tok/s from the first new token to the last, best of 5.
  • Roofline: a decode step reads the 8.04 GB of weights and ~160 MB of KV cache, 11.1 ms or 90 tok/s. Prefill does 7.8 TFLOP (7.44 in the GEMMs, 0.32 in attention).

Expect ±3 % between runs (the GPU also drives the desktop).

CUDA graphs

Decode steps (≤ 8 tokens) are captured once and replayed, one launch instead of ~365 (10 kernels per layer, plus 5); pipelined, the host samples a step behind the GPU, so it enqueues the next step before reading the token. Prefill steps run eagerly in every configuration.

Decode, tok/sfused kernelsone kernel per op
eager steps76.472.3
replayed from a graph, step by step77.9 (1.02×)75.2 (1.04×)
replayed and pipelined (the engine)78.7 (1.03×)75.9 (1.05×)

A 4B step takes 12.7 ms, long enough for the host to enqueue most launches while earlier kernels run; graphs remove what is left, 0.25 ms per token, and pipelining another 0.13 ms, the host's read of the token. On 0.6B (2.5 ms per step), graphs gained 1.22×.

Fused kernels, FlashAttention and PagedAttention

From one plain op per kernel to the engine's kernels, with eager steps so that each row changes one thing:

PrefillDecode
Reference ops (ref): a broadcast or cuBLAS call per op; attention gathers the pages into dense K and V and materializes the scores458 ms8.6 tok/s
cuTile kernel per op (GEMV/GEMM, RMSNorm, RoPE, KV write, SwiGLU), same attention (unfused-refattn-eager)353 ms (1.30×)11.2 tok/s (1.30×)

The reference attention runs about 15 kernels per KV head and layer and copies the cache into dense matrices every step; for prefill it writes the 1,024 × 1,024 score matrix of every head. The attention kernels read each page once from the pool through the block table.

The fused kernels, bit-identical to the chains of kernels they replace:

  • attention inputs: q/k RMSNorm, RoPE and the KV cache write straight from the fused q/k/v projection, 2 kernels instead of 8 (3 copies, 2 norms, 2 RoPE, 1 write);
  • the residual add with the next RMSNorm;
  • SwiGLU in the epilogue of the decode gate_up GEMV, and Float32 logits from the lm_head GEMV.

With graphs and pipelining, the fused kernels still gain 1.04× on decode (75.9 → 78.7 tok/s): ~0.45 ms per token of memory traffic and kernel tails.

Not kept: the attention combine fused into the split-KV kernel (the last split of a head merges, through an acquire-release counter): 2 % slower than kernel + combine.

Prefill GEMMs

The GEMMs are ~87 % of a prefill step's GPU time. gemm_config picks, per shape, the tile among 6 that minimizes waves × (TM TN)^0.7 (fitted to a sweep of 30 tiles on both models' layers at n = 2…1,024: within 2 % of the best tile on average), and grids of ≥ 2 blocks per SM run two blocks per SM (gemm_occupancy, 1.06× on a 512-token prefill). Against cuBLAS for every linear layer (gemmEx!, Float32 accumulation):

cuTilecuBLAS
Prefill85.6 ms (91 TFLOP/s)82.8 ms (94 TFLOP/s)
Decode (GEMVs)78.7 tok/s78.7 tok/s

Not kept: split-K (Float32 partials and a reduce kernel) for n = 2…64 never beat the best direct tile; the latency hint on the GEMM loads.

INT8 weights

Weight-only INT8 with one Float32 scale per output channel (quantize = :int8), the embedding in BF16. Mean KL divergence to the BF16 model over the fixture prompts 0.009, top-1 agreement 49/50.

BF16INT8 weights
Prefill85.6 ms90.9 ms (0.94×)
Decode78.7 tok/s138.2 tok/s (1.76×)

Decode reads half the bytes. The INT8 GEMVs want half BF16's tile width (TK × TM ≈ 4,096 Int8 elements). Prefill stays compute-bound, and the Int8 weight tile is converted in registers rather than staged through shared memory like the BF16 operands (1.6× the instructions of the BF16 GEMM); gemm_int8_config has its own tiles, which took INT8 prefill on short prompts from 8–11 % slower than BF16 to 1.5× faster (64 tokens) but leave it 6 % behind at 1,024.

INT8 also fits models that BF16 does not: Apertus 1.5 8B (16.1 GB in BF16) runs in 8.6 GB at ~80 tok/s.

INT8 KV cache

One Int8 scale per cached vector, keys smoothed by the key norm's weight (KV cache), which brings the mean KL to a BF16 cache from 0.030 to 0.0051 (Qwen3-0.6B, chat prompt).

At 1,024 positions the cache is 2 % of the bytes a step reads, so halving it gains nothing: the INT8 cache pays off as the cache grows next to the weights (BF16 cache: 1.2 GB at 8k, 4.8 GB at 32k, against 8 GB of BF16 weights or 4 GB of INT8 ones), and fits more context. The long-context rows are bench/run.jl --context 8192 and --context 32768 (the decode benchmark after that many cached positions). To keep the attention kernels spill-free with the conversion, they load 32-key tiles and each half of the head dimension separately (decode attention at 32k positions: 150 → 119 µs).

Speculative decoding

Each step verifies k drafted tokens in one forward pass of k + 1 tokens, whose cost is close to a 1-token step's, and keeps the longest accepted prefix: the tokens of plain decoding, up to BF16 rounding. Best k of each proposer:

Decode, tok/sedit (copies the input)explain (open text)
plain78.778.7
n-gram lookup in the context396 (5.03×, k = 7), 5.3 tokens per step83 (1.05×)
Qwen3-0.6B draft model235 (2.99×, k = 7), 7.1113 (1.44×, k = 2), 2.1
DFlash block drafter419 (5.33×, k = 15), 7.1174 (2.22×, k = 15), 2.9

DFlash drafts a block of 15 tokens in one pass of a small model over the target's hidden states; a longer block costs nothing more to draft and little more to verify, so the whole block wins even on open text, where per-draft acceptance falls from 0.58 at k = 2 to 0.14 at k = 15 (1.68× and 2.22×). With sampling, drafts are verified by the p/q rule; the picture is the same (Speculative decoding).

Attention for verify steps. Steps of 2–16 tokens (verify steps, and DFlash's pass over its block) run the split-KV decode kernels, with 8 splits from 9 tokens on, instead of the prefill kernel, which runs one block per head over every page (16 tokens at 1,024 positions: 9.5 µs against 27 per layer):
