Speculative decoding

At batch size 1, a decode step reads every weight of the model to produce one token, and the GPU's compute units mostly wait for memory. A step of 8 or 16 tokens reads the same weights once and costs little more. Speculative decoding exploits that: a cheap proposer guesses the next k tokens, and one verify step of the model on all of them keeps the guesses up to the first wrong one, plus the model's own token at that point. Each step emits 1 to k + 1 tokens for about the price of one.

Code: src/engine/speculative.jl (verify steps, the generation loop, n-gram and draft-model proposers) and src/engine/dflash.jl (the DFlash proposer).

generate(model, ids; sampling, stop_ids, speculative = NgramProposer(k = 4))

or --speculate K, --draft MODEL or --dflash DRAFTER on the command line.

One speculative step

With last the latest token, sampled but not yet run through the model:

  1. Propose drafts d₁ … dₖ to follow the context.
  2. Verify: run [last, d₁, …, dₖ] through the model in one step of k + 1 tokens. The step computes the logits of every position and samples the model's own choice tᵢ after each prefix, all on the device.
  3. Accept drafts while dᵢ = tᵢ. If the first a drafts match, the step emits d₁ … dₐ and then tₐ₊₁, the model's own token where the drafts went wrong (or after the last draft): the "bonus" token, which becomes the next last.
  4. Roll back: truncate! drops the keys and values of the rejected drafts from the cache. Their pages go back to the pool and later steps overwrite their slots, so this costs nothing on the GPU.

Verify steps of each length (up to 16 tokens) are captured as CUDA graphs, like decode steps, and their logits and samples stay on the device: the host reads only the k + 1 sampled tokens.

Why the output does not change

Greedy decoding. tᵢ is the argmax after each prefix, exactly the token plain decoding would pick, so the output is plain greedy decoding's. (In BFloat16 a verify step runs other kernel shapes than a decode step, so the logits can differ in the last bits and the outputs part at near-ties; in Float32 they are identical, which the tests check.)

Sampling. The verify step samples each position with the same sampler and the same counter-based random numbers plain decoding would use for that token. For a deterministic proposer, "sample t from p and accept d if t = d" is exactly rejection sampling (Leviathan et al., Chen et al.) with a one-hot proposal: d is accepted with probability p(d), and otherwise the token comes from p without d. So sampled speculative decoding produces the tokens of plain sampled decoding, not only the same distribution.

Sampled drafts. A model proposer can also sample its drafts from its own distribution q (sample = true, --sample-drafts). Then each drafted position applies the general rule on the device (accept_token!): accept d with probability min(1, p(d)/q(d)), otherwise sample from max(p − q, 0), renormalized. The tokens follow the model's distribution, but are no longer plain decoding's. With Qwen3's recommended sampling the gain was mixed (+8 % on one open prompt, −3 to −12 % on a code prompt), so greedy drafts stay the default.

Draft model. Qwen3-0.6B drafting for Qwen3-4B. Each proposal first rewinds the draft's sequence to what the target accepted, then decodes k − 1 steps as a chain of CUDA graphs: step i samples draft i + 1 on the device straight into the inputs of step i + 1, so the host enqueues the whole chain and waits once. A draft step costs about a fifth of a 4B decode step, so each draft must be accepted with probability ~0.2 or more to pay, and k = 2 to 4 is best.

DFlash (Chen et al., 2026), with z-lab's Qwen3-4B-DFlash-b16. The target's steps keep the residual stream after five of its layers (ModelRunner does this when a DFlash proposer is attached). The drafter is five Qwen3 layers: the keys and values of context positions come from a projection of those target states and stay in the drafter's own cache, and a block of 16 positions (the next token, then 15 mask tokens) attends to the whole context and to itself without a causal mask. The target's own lm_head over the block gives 15 drafts at once. A drafter pass costs 3.2 ms on Qwen3-4B, a 16-token verify 13.3 ms against 12.7 ms for one token: a step costs ~1.3 decode steps and pays from 1.3 tokens per step on. The drafts are written on the device into the verify step's inputs, so the host waits once per step.

Unlike chained drafters, whose acceptance falls along the chain, a longer DFlash block costs nothing more to draft and little more to verify, so drafting the whole block (k = 15) wins on short contexts, even though per-draft acceptance drops.

An EAGLE-3 proposer (one drafter layer on three target layers' states, chained one token at a time) was built and removed: DFlash, which reads the same kind of states, beat it on every prompt (on 256-token chat replies, 1.45× to 2.25× against DFlash's 1.92× to 8.50×).

On the open explanation, sampled drafts are accepted less far: DFlash's best k drops to 4 (k = 15: 2.01×), and on earlier 2.2k-token prompts k = 7 beat the whole block even greedily, so the best k depends on the text and the context.

Not built yet

  • Adaptive k: choose k, or the proposer, per step from the measured acceptance rate and step costs, as DeepInfer's scheduler does. The sampled results above show what it would gain.
  • Tree verification: verify several candidate continuations in one step with a tree attention mask, as EAGLE and SpecInfer do.
